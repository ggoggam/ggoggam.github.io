A colleague in the lab recently introduced me to GFlowNets[^1], short for Generative Flow Networks. I was excited by the idea: extend reinforcement learning (RL) to produce a variety of useful results.
A GFlowNet builds an object through a series of steps and samples from a distribution with several peaks, aiming to produce objects with high rewards.

Conventional RL usually tries to maximize reward. That can lead it to keep producing results from only a few peaks in the distribution, a problem called mode collapse.
GFlowNets aim for a wider variety of high-reward results. This makes them useful when we want several new candidates, such as molecules for possible drug discovery, an application explored in the original paper[^2].

## Building an object step by step

A GFlowNet learns a policy, a rule for choosing the next step, that creates an object.

## Definitions

We define a Markov Decision Process (MDP) as a tuple $\langle S, A, f, r, \rho_0 \rangle$. Here, $S$ is the set of states, $A$ is the set of actions, $f: S \to S$ describes transitions between states, $r$ gives the reward, and $\rho_0$ is the initial distribution of states.
